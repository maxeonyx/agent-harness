For the harness · the Underview writeup

Why Underview exists, as stories

Underview, the scheduler, the planner, the coach and the stand-in were each built up one correction at a time. This page pulls out the needs behind them as user stories, for you and for your agents, each with the episode that showed the need. It's written generically, with no work specifics, so it can go into the harness as the requirements behind its graph view.

Each story states a need, then Because: the concrete episode that made it a need. Where you said it yourself, your words follow. Quotes are verbatim, and anything in [brackets] is mine, made generic. A gold margin is a story for you. An orange one is for an agent, or for the harness itself.

125 stories in 21 sections: 87 for you and 38 for agents. Sections 1 to 11 are the concepts you listed. Sections 12 to 19 are needs I found along the way, and sections 20 and 21 are about carrying them into a graph. The decisions the graph design has to make are in Tensions still open, each with my recommendation.

Six needs underneath all of it

Almost every story traces back to one of these. If a design choice in the harness serves them, it will probably satisfy the stories too.

Your attention is the scarcest thing

Every node, question and page competes for it. Gold, the stand-in and "only what's really yours" all exist to spend it well.

You forget, so the system mustn't

Dates, promises, chores, context and why each thing matters. You can't ask about what you've forgotten.

Agents' accounts go stale, and sources don't

Whatever a machine can check, the machine says. Agents say only what only they know.

Code holds rules, and agents don't

A rule agents must remember is followed unevenly. A check on write, a watch or a node type holds every time.

The loud thing isn't the important thing

In the moment you're pulled toward what's in front of you. The plan, the scheduler and the coach pull toward what you chose.

Agents drift toward agreeing with you

So judgement lives outside the conversation, decisions travel only through a log, and the stand-in is tested against your real redirections.

How the parts fit

Each role exists because it needed to run at a different pace, or keep a different interest, from the others.

The planner runs weekly and when decisions arrive. The scheduler runs often. The coach answers in under a minute because both have already done the heavy work.

Terms

Underview's own name is in brackets where the generic name differs, and each ends with the nearest term in the harness's glossary. Everything here is per domain, in the harness's sense: work and home each get their own.

Node
Anything on the graph: a PR, an issue, a question, an action, a planned step, a session. Harness: a task, or a question or user action node, on the map.
Stream
One agent's own region of the graph: the facts about its work that only it knows. Harness: no equivalent yet; nearest is an area's work under its main agent.
Move
Whose turn a node is: yours, an agent's, someone else's, or nobody's yet. Harness: no equivalent yet; partly Waiting::Max.
Gold
The few nodes that are your moves, picked and ordered by the scheduler. Harness: the front of the attention scheduler's queue.
Source
A system that owns some state: the code host, a ticketing system, deploy records, a calendar. Harness: read through watches.
Planner
Keeps your commitments and the steps toward them. Runs weekly, and when decisions arrive. Harness: no equivalent yet.
Scheduler
Fits the plan and every stream into today's order (the prioritizer). Harness: the attention scheduler, one per domain.
Coach
The one agent you talk to about your day. It owns no work. Harness: no equivalent yet.
Stand-in
Answers as you would, from your own words (max-advocate). Its search over your words is max-view. Harness: no equivalent yet.
Decision log
Where your decisions are written down. It's the planner's only input from conversation. Harness: no equivalent yet; the scheduler "learns from his disagreement" is the nearest.
Coordinator
Keeps the agents running: restarts stalled ones, chases stale streams, adopts orphan work. Harness: mostly the harness itself, since it runs the agents and knows when one stops.

Gold: what I do next

Gold is the short, numbered list of my next actions, picked from everything in flight. It exists because my attention goes where the pull is strongest, not where I'd put it on reflection, and a dozen agents make every pull stronger. Most of the stories here are corrections: each one is a way a plausible ranking went wrong.

Max

The first few actions are already picked when I arrive

As Max, I want to open the graph and see my first actions numbered in gold, so that I never spend attention working out "what now?"

Because"What now?" costs attention every time I ask it, and agents have made starting work almost free, so there's always more than I can hold. Without an answer waiting for me I pick whatever feels most urgent, which is rarely what pays most.
special gold styling (not important, but fun!)21 Oct
I personally am not necessarily very good at prioritizing my time across all of the threads that need my input.On the harness vision
Max

Only my moves are gold

As Max, I want gold to hold only things I can act on right now, so that I never open a gold item and find it's waiting on someone else.

BecauseA ranking put a PR that was waiting on a teammate's review in gold, on the grounds that nudging them would help. That's a different action. If a nudge is worth my time, it gets its own item, and the waiting PR stays out of gold. A thing that hasn't started yet can't be gold either.
It's literally waiting on [a teammate] [...] If it's not a max action, I don't think it can be gold.23 Oct
Max

Gold is true at the moment I look

As Max, I want a gold item that's already done to disappear by itself, so that I don't pay twice for every stale pick: once to read it, once to correct it.

BecauseOn 21 Oct all five of the coordinator's top picks had already merged. On 23 Oct three of the top four were done. On 29 Oct the coach named a PR as my next step an hour after I'd merged it. About half my messages to the coach that week were corrections like these. A short list that's all true is worth more than a long one that's mostly true, and enough wrong items and I stop looking.
If I've stopped looking, it's because it stopped working.27 Oct
Max

Ranked by what it's really worth, not how loud it is

As Max, I want items weighed by the importance of the end goal and whether the work is really mine, so that something that looks urgent can't jump the queue on looks alone.

BecauseOne morning's first item was "add four addresses to a summary email", ranked urgent. Nobody had checked who reads that email or why it mattered: it went to the whole company, and it was my manager's work anyway. Unknown importance isn't high importance. A failing job matters only as much as the job does.
There's a job failing, but if the job's not important [...] it's just not important.On ranking
Max

Tiny things that move work along go first

As Max, I want quick items, like a merge someone has already approved, to open my morning and my afternoon, so that I start each half of the day by getting things moving.

BecauseA ten-second merge that unblocks someone has an enormous value for its cost, and it's exactly the kind of thing I forget. "Quick" is relative, though: across 27 quick items, the median took me about four minutes, and an agent's "0.2-minute" yes/no took a median of 5.8, because I have to load its context first. Ten yes/no questions is an hour, not a coffee break.
If it's tiny effort, it's high leverage. And it should go first, why not?On ranking
Max

A teammate blocked on me goes first, whatever its size

As Max, I want a teammate whose own work can't move until I review, answer or decide to come before my project work, even when it's a big review, so that nobody on my team waits days on me.

BecauseA teammate's 8,000-line review sat at the bottom of my list for three days: it wasn't quick, so it went into the long tail. Another teammate's stack of PRs, open for two months, ranked 15th to 17th. Meanwhile most of what waits on me is my own agents' work, which is a different kind of waiting. A teammate blocked on me is a person who can't work. An agent blocked on me is a process. A handful of teammate items a week shouldn't lose to that flood.
In general, unblocking my team is always high priority.29 Oct
Max

My long, important work doesn't starve

As Max, I want my most important long project to get a protected share of my project time, so that the short jobs that keep arriving can't push it out every single day.

BecauseMy top goal for the year went nine months without passing three PRs a month. Every morning brought new reviews and questions, each short, each with someone waiting, each with a better value-for-time than a long piece of work nobody was waiting on. So the long work lost, every day. In scheduling theory that's starvation, and the standard fix is a reserved share.
Max

It tells me what I'm not doing today

As Max, I want to see what was considered for today and decided against, with why, so that something left undone at five o'clock is a decision, not a failure.

BecauseThere's always more in flight than a day holds. Without an explicit "not today", the leftovers read as things I failed to do, which is demotivating, and nobody can see that a choice was made.
I need a process that can actually plan my year's work, my month's work, my week's work, and finally my day's work and tell me what I'm not doing today. Right, importantly.27 Oct
Max

Older wins, but only between near-equals

As Max, I want age to break ties between items of about the same priority, so that old work finishes and stops sitting in my head, without old low-priority work rising by age alone.

BecauseUnfinished work keeps intruding on whatever I'm doing, and finishing three things frees more attention than advancing nine. But some old work is old because it was deliberately put off, and that isn't "all else equal". Work I've parked doesn't age at all.
If something seems equally priority, then old should win. Or even slightly different in priority, but only slightly, then old should win.27 Oct
Max

No daily target, and I'll do things out of order

As Max, I want the list overfilled, with no target, and to be free to do items in my own order, so that the list stays a priority, not a contract I fail to keep.

BecauseA daily target makes me feel bad when I miss it, and when I hit it I relax rather than pick up more. When I'm in one agent's session I'll answer all its questions, not just the top one, which batches unplanned items with a planned one. That's efficient, and I need to be able to do it without it counting as going off plan.
I'm not interested in having a target that I need to hit each day. It's counter to my psychology.27 Oct
Max

Something I enjoy, every day

As Max, I want one enjoyable or tidying item in each day, so that I don't come to hate a list that's only obligations.

BecauseThe work I'm drawn to is a real source of energy, and taking it away completely backfires. Pairing it after the hard work is a reward instead of a distraction.
Otherwise I'm just doing what my KPIs say and I'm just gonna hate it. So there's a real balance to be hit here.27 Oct
Max

Each pick says why, in a line I can act on

As Max, I want each gold item's reason to be what I'd do and what it releases, and the day's summary to be at most three short lines, so that I read the reasoning in a glance or not at all.

Because"You OK'd it, [a teammate] approved it on the 20th: merge" tells me what to do. "Unblocks the release" doesn't. The first ranking summary was a paragraph, and I didn't read it. The reasoning behind the ranking is useful to the agents that keep the system going, and belongs where they read it, not on my screen.
It's a giant paragraph of plain text in small text. So it's just not appealing. [...] it needs to be very small.On the ranking summary
Max

A due date informs gold, it doesn't decide it

As Max, I want dates shown on everything that has one, but ranked by judgement, so that a date makes me aware without automatically making something my top priority.

BecauseFor one day, anything due or urgent joined gold automatically, and gold filled with things that weren't the best use of my time. Dates are a mechanical fact the page can show by itself. Priority is a judgement.
There are two different sets. There's due dates, which we mechanically know, due soon or overdue. But then there's also your prioritization. [...] Doesn't always mean it's gold. Often does, but doesn't always.22 Oct
Every agent

Agents give the facts, one ranker weighs them

As an agent working on Max's behalf, I want to publish the facts that ranking needs (a date, why it matters, whose move it is, how long it takes him) and never a priority, so that one ranker can compare everything on the same scale.

BecauseEvery agent thinks its own work matters most. When the coordinator ranked while it was also relaying between thirty-odd agents, it anchored on whatever each had last told it. The fix was a separate ranker that starts fresh, isn't in the conversation, and treats anything in an agent's data that reads like a priority as a claim to check.
Priority is your job, not the agent's job. They need to provide those factors.On stream data
The scheduler

Check every claim, and look beyond what agents report

As the scheduler, I want to check each item against the real thing and to search for work that no agent lists, so that I surface what Max doesn't know he's missing.

BecauseAn agent's data is its last account of its work, and it's often out of date: a PR still listed as waiting on a teammate who approved it two days ago. And the work Max forgets is exactly the work no agent is tracking. The first time the page joined everything, it found 13 of his open PRs on no stream, two review requests no agent knew of, and nine sessions nobody owned. Reviews he started and never submitted, and approvals waiting on his merge, are the same kind of thing.
The unknown unknowns are the thing I need you to be servicing to me.On the scheduler
The scheduler

Weigh what Max's minute releases

As the scheduler, I want to rank an answer that sets an agent off on hours of work above one that only closes a card, so that Max's minutes go where they release the most work.

BecauseOne day his top project's agent reported at 09:16 and sat idle until 16:40, and he got no work done on that project. Ranking by his minutes alone treats every answer as equal, though some unblock hours of agent work and others unblock nothing.
I feel like we haven't prioritized setting off agents on long work streams.29 Oct
The scheduler

Correct agents' estimates, privately

As the scheduler, I want to correct agents' time estimates when I plan Max's day, but never show him a corrected figure, so that the plan is realistic and he sees one estimate, not arithmetic.

BecauseAgents' guesses at Max's hands-on time run about four times low. The ×4 correction leaked into his messages, a draft one-pager even multiplied a guess into a date, and he worried it would be applied twice. Estimates he gives himself, or that a planner made at his real pace, are already right and must not be corrected again.
It doesn't need to tell me about the times four. That's just too much information. It's an estimate or it's not, right?27 Oct
The harness

Mechanical updates need no agent

As the harness, I want to drop finished items, add a new review request, and show a due date without waking any agent, so that the ranking stays current between runs and costs nothing to maintain.

BecauseMost of what changes the day is mechanical: a merge, a review requested at 14:00, a date arriving. An agent re-ranking for each of these costs usage and adds another thing that can be wrong. A mistyped pick also once dropped out of gold silently, so the harness has to say out loud when a pick matches nothing.
I merge a PR. The build updates the page. From GitHub, it knows that item is done. The prioritizer doesn't need to run.29 Oct

Dependencies

A dependency says one item can't finish until another has. They're what make the page a graph instead of a list, and they're how the scheduler knows what an answer unblocks.

Max

I see what waits on what as arrows, not words

As Max, I want dependencies drawn as arrows, including between projects, so that I see at a glance what unblocks what, without reading.

BecauseHover cards that said "Waits on" and "Then" were junk next to the arrows. When two projects' items were merged into one island to keep their links short, it became very hard to read. Separate islands with lines crossing between them read correctly. Hovering an item highlights its neighbours. Arrows fade slightly where they pass under another project.
I'd much prefer separate islands, with lines crossing between them, if you can do that. That would be the correct visual grammar.21 Oct
Max

Arrows always mean the same thing

As Max, I want every arrow to point the same way, from the dependent to what it depends on, with a solid head, so that I never have to work out which way one is pointing.

BecauseAgents drew them backwards again and again, and drew hollow arrowheads that lines ran over. Two waits in opposite directions drawn as two lines covered each other's heads. A visual language only works if it never varies, and a graph whose arrows I have to decode is worse than a list.
Oh, your arrows are the wrong way around, mate. [...] dependent to dependency.6 Oct
Max

Old work never comes to depend on new work

As Max, I want to start new things freely but be stopped from making older work wait on newer work, so that new ideas can't quietly push back things already in flight.

BecauseA new idea is always more exciting than the work it would delay. When the old work comes to depend on it, the old work stops finishing, and nobody decided that. Starting something new, or scheduling it privately for later, costs the old work nothing.
This process doesn't need to stop me opening new work, by the way, but it does need to stop me making the [old] work depend on the new work. Scheduling stuff for later privately is fine.27 Oct
Max

Planned work shows, faintly

As Max, I want work an agent plans but hasn't started (a future PR, a design step, a question it will need to ask) to show as provisional nodes, so that I can see and steer the plan before it becomes real work.

BecauseThe plan is where I most want to give feedback, and it's cheap to change before anything is built. A planned node must not look like the thing it will become: a planned step drawn with the draft-PR icon falsely said "this will be a PR". Dashed for planned, solid for in progress, is how I read them.
A work agent

Link to the exact item I wait on, in another agent's work

As an agent, I want to say my item waits on a specific item of another agent's, or on a PR or issue by its URL, so that the dependency exists even though neither of us owns the other's work.

BecauseA dependency between two agents' work is visible only if one of them writes it down. A link to a whole project says nothing about what has to finish. A link to an item can be checked: if it points at nothing, the harness says so, so a typo can't hide it.
A work agent

What I'm waiting on Max for is its own node

As an agent whose next step waits on Max confirming something, I want that confirmation to be a question node that's his move, with my waiting items depending on it and nobody's move, so that the graph shows exactly what his answer will unblock.

Because"Waiting" said in words is invisible to ranking. A node with arrows behind it lets the scheduler see that one yes from Max releases five items of work, and rank it that way.
A work agent

A deploy-time step depends on the deploy, not the merge

As an agent, I want a step that happens at release time (a migration, a production run) to hang off the release, with the release's date, so that nobody runs it when the code merges, before anything is deployed.

BecauseAn agent tied a migration to a PR's merge. Merging changes nothing in production. The migration has to run with the release that ships the code, and between merge and release it would run against a system that doesn't expect it.
The migration goes with the release, not with the merge!!On a release

Action types

Every node has a kind: a PR to review, an issue, a question, a task for me, an agent working, planned work, a chore, something waiting on the outside world. Kinds exist because each one asks something different of me and opens somewhere different when I click it.

Max

As Max, I want each node's kind and whose move it is to be visible without reading or a legend, so that I take in the whole graph at a glance and skip everything that isn't mine.

BecauseAn early page needed a key, which meant its visual language was wrong. Coloured text and status words ("Approved by [a teammate]: merge it") were noise where a tick says the same thing. Underview met this with shape for kind (a question took the diagram convention for a decision) and fill for whose move, because fill is read before anything else.
I don't think the key is really required; if it is, our visual language is wrong.21 Oct

A question is answered to the agent, so it opens the page that spells the question out, or the agent's session. It never opens the code it's about. Clicking a project's title opens that project's page.
A card's primary link should go to the place that I can action it.29 Oct
Max

An action arrives ready to do

Once I've handed it on, it's off my plate

As Max, I want an action to leave my list as soon as I've done my part, such as marking a PR ready and asking a colleague to review it, and to come back only when it's mine again, so that I carry only what's really mine.

BecauseThe work isn't finished when I act, but my part is. The agent waits on the colleague's review, deals with their comments, and hands the PR back to me only when it's approved and mine to merge. Checking that a step happened (a review requested, an approval) is a job for code watching the source, not for me or an agent.
[Once it's marked ready and a colleague's review is requested,] it's no longer my problem.31 Oct, harness vision

Things waiting on the outside world sit quietly

BecauseIf they're not on the graph I lose track of them, and if they're prominent they compete for attention they don't need. Underview greyed such a card and marked it with the other party, which said "known, not yours yet".
Max

"Agent working" is only shown when it's true

As Max, I want a node to show an agent working only while it really is, so that I can trust that what looks like progress is progress.

BecauseA Markdown file is neither a designed page nor part of the conversation. If the agent's message would do, the session is the right place. If not, it should be a page.

A work agent

Unfinished work without a PR still shows

As an agent, I want work on a branch that isn't a PR yet to show as a branch, and to become the PR by itself once one is opened, so that work in progress is visible without a draft PR sitting in Max's list.

There's planned PRs, but there's now built but not surfaced PRs. Work in progress ones.27 Oct

Blocked on who

Every node says whose move it is: mine, a named person's, an agent's, or nobody's. It's the single most useful fact on the page, and the one that goes stale fastest.

Max

I can tell whose move it is before I read anything

As Max, I want to see who holds each node (me, a teammate, an agent, someone outside) at a glance, with one cue per party that means the same thing everywhere, so that my items stand out and everyone else's can be skipped.

Because"Who" is what decides whether a node needs me. In Underview, initials for colleagues and a distinct mark for agents read faster than names, and the same mark everywhere (the agent's colour on its node, its session link and its "stalled" warning) means one cue is learned once. Everyone who isn't me can share a colour.

"Whose move" is checked, not guessed

As Max, I want whose move it is to be checked against what actually happened (who reviewed, who owns the work), not inferred, so that I'm not handed work that isn't mine.

BecauseA release was marked as mine. The coach then guessed it was an agent's, and it was actually a teammate's. A PR was marked as needing my re-review because someone pushed commits after I approved it; on my team we don't re-review on new commits. Ownership guessed from who committed last was wrong more than once.
Since I approved it [...] I don't need to re-review it.On reviews
Max

Work that doesn't need me never reaches me

As Max, I want work that's too unimportant to need my judgement or my access to be settled without me, so that a finished side project doesn't come back to me as a chore.

BecauseA demo from a hackathon that was over came back to me as a PR to merge, a day after I'd handed it off. It needed nobody's judgement. Something stays mine only if it needs my judgement or my access. That's a call about this case, though, not a new standing rule: when the coach turned this one complaint into "demo repos are never Max's", that was an overcorrection.

Max

Nobody else gets an obligation without my say-so

As Max, I want an item that would be a colleague's move to stay a draft until I agree to ask them, so that my agents never put work on another person in my name.

BecauseA review request, an issue, a comment or a meeting each puts work on a person, and it goes out as me. Agents can see that a colleague is the right person long before it's the right moment, or the right way, to ask them. The graph can show the draft. Sending it is my act.
Max

Work that can't start yet is nobody's move

As Max, I want an item that can't begin until a date to be grey, saying when it opens, and nobody's move until then, so that it neither nags me early nor gets lost.

Because"Measure the saving on next month's bill" can't start until the bill is final, weeks away. Shown as my move, it nags. Hidden, it's forgotten. A start date isn't a deadline: putting it in the due field made it look overdue before it could even begin.

BecauseAgents ended their turn on a report and then sat idle with their own next step undone. Nobody's job was to notice. The ones that stopped because Max finished for the day, or because of a usage limit or a crash, stayed stopped until someone happened to look.

As the agent that keeps the whole picture moving, I want to restart any agent whose next move is its own and which has stopped (usage limit, crash, end of Max's day), without asking Max, so that his morning starts with work already moving.

BecauseAgents that are mid-work when Max stops for the day stop with him. Restarting them is mechanical and needs nothing from him. An agent that's waiting on Max stays as it is.

States

A node's state is where it is in its life: planned, in progress on a branch, open for review, approved, done, parked. States exist so that I can see where everything stands without opening it, and so that finished work leaves on its own.

Max

Only really open items are on the page

As Max, I want finished items to leave the graph by themselves, and only genuinely open work to remain, so that everything I see is something that still needs doing.

BecauseAgents kept items open after the work had landed, and I kept telling them to merge PRs I'd already merged. Every item that isn't really open is noise I have to read and dismiss. A finished project folds away into something small rather than vanishing, so I can still find it.
I don't want to deal with open items on your page that aren't really open items.16 Oct

What an interactive graph changes

Underview is a page built from files. Inside the harness, the graph is the system itself. Several needs above were met with workarounds because the page could only display things. This is my read of which ones change, not something you've said.

Tensions still open

Some of these needs pull against each other, and Underview hadn't settled them. They're decisions for the new design.

Everything, or only the signal?

"The page needs to show everything", against "high signal, low noise". My guess: everything is reachable from the graph, and gold stays minimal.

Teammates first, or my block first?

Unblocking a teammate goes first, but long work starves without a reserved block. Underview orders the day by class. A live graph may need an explicit time fence.

Ranked list, or graph layout?

Gold as a list at the top is fast to act on. Gold placed in its graph context shows why. The graph probably needs both: a list that points into the graph.

One coach, or a main agent per area?

Underview has one coach for everything. The harness has a main agent per area. My pick: one coach per domain, because it serves your one attention there and must own no work, and each area's main agent owns that area's work and plan steps.

Chat, or questions and actions as tools?

You've been leaning towards questions and actions as explicit tools. My pick: every agent asks through those tools, so each question is a node, and only the coach stays a conversation, whose output is entries in the decision log rather than chat.

A stand-in that knows enough, but not too much

The stand-in is better the more of your words it reads, and work and home must not mix. My pick: one stand-in per domain, reading only that domain's words. Your general preferences that hold everywhere can live in shared config both read.

Just you, or a team layer later?

You've said you might one day sell your team on the approach, with "one extra layer, for the whole team. That's a pipe dream, but take it into account." My pick: build for one person, but keep facts from sources and from agents apart from your personal ranking, so a team could share the first two.
